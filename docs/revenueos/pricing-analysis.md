# RevenueOS pricing analysis

Date: 2026-08-03

## Conclusion

The proposed pricing is commercially defensible and the earlier low-end studio ranges would be too low for the stated responsibility. RevenueOS implementation is not a form build or CRM configuration. It can include revenue-critical event capture, delivery assurance, CRM/ERP contracts, state migration, reconciliation, sourcing and margin logic, security, deployment, recovery, and operational support.

Commodity CRM list prices are not the correct comparison. A customer often pays separately for CRM seats, CPQ, routing, inventory/ERP, implementation, support, and integration. Official 2026 anchors include Salesforce Revenue Cloud from $200/user/month, Chili Piper routing from $15,000/year, Cin7 from $349/month, Unleashed from $399/month plus modules and onboarding, and custom-priced enterprise CPQ and revenue intelligence.

## Recommended public ranges

| Engagement | Implementation | Ongoing | Commercial purpose |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Revenue Operations Audit | $2,500–$7,500 | — | Fixed-scope evidence and roadmap |
| Pilot | $10,000–$25,000 | Support only if contracted | Prove one representative RFQ path |
| Professional | $25,000–$60,000 | $750–$1,500/month | Several workflows and integrations |
| Business | $60,000–$150,000 | $2,000–$5,000/month | Multi-team CRM/ERP and private-cloud scope |
| Enterprise | $150,000–$500,000+ | Custom annual contract | Multi-company, security, warehouse, migration, SLA |
| Self-hosted first year | $40,000–$250,000+ | Annual license and support | Customer-controlled production boundary |

## Price drivers

- number and quality of RFQ sources;
- monthly RFQ and line-item volume;
- commercial users, teams, regions, and companies;
- website, mailbox, CRM, ERP, supplier, BI, and identity integrations;
- migration and source reconciliation;
- product, currency, cost, margin, quote, approval, and loss logic;
- managed cloud, customer cloud, or self-hosted responsibility;
- roles, SSO, audit, privacy, and security review;
- backup, restore, incident response, support hours, and SLA;
- optional provider-managed AI and its operating controls.

## Commercial rules

1. Publish ranges, not a fake checkout table.
2. Use fixed-scope discovery and milestone implementation.
3. Credit audit fees only when stated in the proposal.
4. Do not include unlimited managed AI.
5. BYO AI provider usage is paid directly by the customer.
6. Self-hosting uses an annual license and mandatory first-year support; implementation is separate.
7. Maintenance should remain approximately 18–25% of the applicable license value where a self-hosted license is used, subject to contract scope.
8. Avoid hourly-only positioning. Hourly rates may exist internally for change control, but the public offer is outcome and milestone based.

## Unit economics guardrail

Every proposal must reserve capacity for discovery, architecture, implementation, integration testing, customer acceptance, release, rollback, documentation, and support handover. If the customer budget cannot cover those responsibilities, reduce module and integration scope rather than discounting the reliability standard.
